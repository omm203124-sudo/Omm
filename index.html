🦴 The Skeleton

An adult human body has 206 bones, while a newborn baby has around 270 softer bones that slowly fuse together as the child grows. The skeleton gives the body its shape, supports our weight, protects soft organs and works with muscles to let us move.

The skull protects the brain, the rib cage protects the heart and lungs, and the spine protects the spinal cord. Bone marrow inside some bones also makes new blood cells, and bones store minerals like calcium.

The femur (thigh bone) is the longest and strongest bone, and the stapes in the ear is the smallest. Each hand has 27 bones and each foot has 26.

🧠 The Brain

The brain is the control centre of the body. It weighs about 1.4 kg and contains roughly 86 billion nerve cells (neurons) that talk to each other using tiny electrical signals. It lets us think, remember, learn, feel emotions and control every movement.

It works with the spinal cord and nerves: they carry information from our eyes, ears, skin and other senses to the brain, and carry its instructions back to the muscles. Even while we sleep, the brain keeps our heart beating and our lungs breathing.

❤️ The Heart

The heart is a muscular pump about the size of a fist, located slightly left of the centre of the chest. It has four chambers and beats around 70 times a minute at rest, which is more than 100,000 times a day.

It pushes blood through blood vessels to every part of the body, delivering oxygen and nutrients and carrying away waste. The blood travels in a loop: heart, lungs, heart again, then the whole body.

🫁 The Lungs

We have two lungs inside the rib cage. When we breathe in, air travels through the windpipe into millions of tiny air sacs called alveoli, where oxygen passes into the blood and carbon dioxide is removed to be breathed out.

An adult breathes roughly 12 to 20 times a minute. The diaphragm, a dome-shaped muscle under the lungs, helps pull air in and push it out.

💪 Muscles and Joints

Muscles are attached to bones by tough cords called tendons. When a muscle contracts, it pulls the bone and moves the joint. Joints like the knee, elbow and shoulder are where two bones meet, and they are cushioned by cartilage and lubricated by fluid.

🍎 Other Important Parts

  • Stomach and intestines break down food and absorb nutrients.
  • Kidneys filter the blood and make urine to remove waste.
  • Liver cleans the blood and helps digest fats.
  • Skin is the largest organ and protects us from germs.
  • Blood carries oxygen and fights infection; an adult has about 5 litres.

🤖 Body Meets Robotics

Just like servo motors move the robot's joints, muscles pull on bones to move our joints. The brain acts like the robot's controller, and nerves are like its wires. Studying the human body helps engineers design better robots, artificial limbs and medical machines.
